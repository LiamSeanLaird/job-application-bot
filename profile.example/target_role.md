# Target role

The AI reads this whole document for every job it checks.
Write it for a reader who doesn't know you: what job you want, why you fit, and how to judge a posting.
The more specific it is, the better the fit scores and reasons get.

## The role in one paragraph

I want a go-to-market (GTM) engineering or revenue operations engineering role.
The job is to build automations and AI tools that sales, marketing and customer success teams use every day, and to work directly with those teams to find what to build.

## Job titles

- GTM Engineer, Go-to-Market Engineer
- Revenue Operations Engineer, RevOps Engineer
- AI Automation Engineer, Business Automation Engineer
- Forward Deployed Engineer, when the work is with business teams

## Strong signals

- Builds workflows across a CRM, data warehouse and outreach tools.
- Works directly with sales, marketing or customer success leaders.
- Uses LLMs or AI agents in production for business teams.
- Measures results, for example with pipeline, conversion or time saved.

## Weak signals

- CRM administration with little building.
- Mostly reporting or dashboards.

## Background

- 4 years as a software engineer at a SaaS startup, building internal tools and LLM features.
- 2 years in a data consulting role, building customer data pipelines and campaign segmentation.
- Comfortable in Python, TypeScript, SQL and one cloud platform.

## Gaps

- No hands-on experience with HubSpot or Salesforce administration yet.
- Never carried a sales quota.

## Screening rules

The AI fit check follows these rules for every job.

- Say yes when business or go-to-market teams use what the person builds, and the job combines AI, automation or data with working directly with those teams.
- Say no to pure software engineering, pure data science, quota-carrying sales, CRM administration with no building, and senior leadership roles that are mainly people management.
- A candidate gap does not by itself mean no, because the candidate is pivoting into this field.
